You're Cheating On God

A Bible study on spiritual adultery, agreement, and the proposals you say I do to

Start session 1 Watch the teaching

The big idea

If Christ is the Bridegroom and we are the bride, then the deepest adultery is not only a body in the wrong bed — it is a mind that says I do to the enemy's report about salvation and identity. You become one with what you agree with. Temptation is a proposal; consent is the wedding. You do not lose the deed of salvation by a thought, but you can walk like a beggar instead of an heir until you marry the finished work again with your mouth.

How to use this study

  1. Pray the opening prayer. This is not a podcast recap. It is a conversation with the Husband the sermon is about.
  2. Watch the timestamped clip, then read the speaker's words slowly. Do not skip the close reading — he packed a whole theology into slang and wedding pictures.
  3. Sit with the verses themselves. We print them, then take them apart word by word so you are not stuck with a vibe.
  4. Answer the questions in writing if you can. The answers are there when you are stuck, not so you can cheat yourself out of thinking.
  5. Do the 'live it' lines before the next session. Agreement is the whole point. Reading about I do is not yet I do.

Scripture quoted in the King James Version, matching the preacher's cadence.

Opening prayer

Father, You are the Maker who is also the Husband. We come as a people You already claimed, not as shoppers. Where we have trusted the enemy's thoughts more than Your speech, expose the vow without crushing the child. Teach us to refuse the ring, to confess sin without marrying shame, and to say I do to what Jesus finished. Let God be true, and every other word a liar. In the name of the Bridegroom, amen.

Read with both ears. This study walks with one teacher's interpretation. We keep his point, name his asides (Koine, tetelestai, Numbers 36 as pattern), and let the verses speak in their own context so you can test everything (Acts 17:11). Physical adultery is still sin. Confessing actual sin is still required. Feeling dirty after failure is not the same as being fathered by the devil. If you are in a real marriage crisis or in despair, tell a pastor or a friend — do not only fight in your head.
