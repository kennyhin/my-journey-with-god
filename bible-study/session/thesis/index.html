True Adultery Is Cheating on the Husband

Christ is the Bridegroom. The enemy is not a hobby — he is a rival.

Watch this moment

What he said

True adultery is cheating on the husband with the enemy. So he is the bridegroom. We are the bride. We're one wife of God basically. And then we turn around and we trust the thoughts and the words of the enemy. Every thought from the enemy is like Satan on his knees with the ring out.

The teacher is not starting with bedroom sin. He is starting with covenant. In Scripture, God marries His people. Jesus is the Bridegroom; the church is the bride. If that is the marriage, then the deepest betrayal is not only a physical affair — it is taking the enemy's thoughts into the bed of your mind and treating them as more intimate than what your Husband already said about you.

Every loaded word

Not every “the” and “and” — the claims, pictures, and slang that carry the sermon.

“True adultery”

He is ranking spiritual unfaithfulness as the real target of the teaching. Physical adultery is still sin. He is saying the covenant God is most jealous for is the one between Christ and His people.

Why it matters. If you only hear 'don't sleep around,' you can pass this message and still be married to shame, the world's verdict, and the devil's whispers.

“cheating on the husband with the enemy”

Husband = Christ. Enemy = Satan. Cheating = giving the enemy the loyalty, agreement, and intimacy that belong to Jesus.

Why it matters. You do not have to like Satan to cheat. You only have to trust his version of you more than Christ's.

“bridegroom / bride / one wife of God”

Biblical marriage language for God and His people (Isaiah 54:5, Ephesians 5, Revelation 19). 'One wife' is his way of saying the church is not a harem of competing identities — we are one body belonging to one Husband.

Why it matters. Identity is not a solo self-help project. You already belong. The question is whether you live like you do.

“trust the thoughts and the words of the enemy”

Trust here is not a feeling. It is agreement. What you treat as true about you becomes the voice you sleep next to.

Why it matters. Most of us would never bow to a statue, but we will rehearse 'I'm still just a mess' until it sounds like gospel.

“Satan on his knees with the ring out”

A picture: temptation is not only an attack. It is a proposal. A ring is an offer of union.

Why it matters. If a thought is a proposal, you are not helpless. You can refuse the ring without pretending the knee never bent.

Verses on the table

Isaiah 54:5 KJV

For thy Maker is thine husband; the LORD of hosts is his name; and thy Redeemer the Holy One of Israel; The God of the whole earth shall he be called.

Word by word

  • MakerThe One who formed you is not a distant boss. He claims husband rights — provision, covering, jealousy, covenant.
  • husbandHebrew ba'al in this verse: owner/husband. Not abuse. Covenant belonging. God is not dating Israel. He married her.
  • RedeemerThe Husband also buys back. Marriage and rescue are the same God. You are not loved because you stayed pretty. You are loved because He paid.
How the speaker uses this verse

This is the Old Testament root of 'He is the Bridegroom.' The speaker's 'one wife of God' sits on this covenant, not on a cute metaphor.

Wider context — keep the verse honest

Isaiah 54 is spoken to a barren, ashamed people after judgment. God does not say, 'Try harder and I'll take you back.' He says, 'Your Maker is your husband.' Grace re-marries the ones who failed.

2 Corinthians 11:2–3 KJV

For I am jealous over you with godly jealousy: for I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ. But I fear, lest by any means, as the serpent beguiled Eve through his subtilty, so your minds should be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ.

Word by word

  • jealousPaul's jealousy is 'godly' — it copies God's. Jealousy here is covenant fire, not insecurity.
  • espousedGreek harmozo: to join, fit, betroth. The church is already pledged. The wedding language is present tense loyalty, not a future maybe.
  • one husbandNot many voices. Not Jesus plus the world's verdict. One. That is the speaker's whole sermon in two words.
  • chaste virginNot 'people who never sinned.' It is undivided devotion — a mind not sleeping around with other gospels.
  • minds should be corruptedThe affair happens in the mind. Eve is the pattern: a conversation, not a temple of idols.
How the speaker uses this verse

This is the verse he will quote later as 'espoused to one husband… presented as a chaste virgin.' Session 1 is the headline; this is the warrant.

Wider context — keep the verse honest

Paul is fighting false apostles, not dating advice. Spiritual adultery in this chapter is receiving another Jesus, another spirit, another gospel (2 Cor 11:4).

Ephesians 5:25–27, 31–32 KJV

Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it… That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle… This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church.

Word by word

  • gave himselfThe marriage was paid for by death. You did not win Him. He spent Himself.
  • washing of water by the wordThe Husband cleanses with speech. What He says about you is the bath, not what the timeline says.
  • mystery… Christ and the churchHuman marriage is the illustration. The real marriage is Jesus and His people.
How the speaker uses this verse

When he says 'we are the bride,' he is standing in Paul's mystery, not inventing a new religion of romance language.

Wider context — keep the verse honest

Christ presents the church to Himself. The bride's beauty is His work. That will matter when the speaker later says you are holy because He did not fail.

Sit with this

Try the question before you open the answer. The answer is a companion, not a score key.

Question 1 · click to revealIf someone followed you around for a week and only heard the sentences you say about yourself, who would they think you are married to — Christ, or the accuser?

Why this question hits

We can sing about Jesus on Sunday and still narrate ourselves as unlovable, unfinished, and disqualified all week. That narration is a relationship.

A way to answer

The honest answer is usually mixed. Most believers have a marriage certificate with Jesus and a secret second relationship with shame. The speaker's point is not 'you never struggle.' It is: the voice you keep saying yes to is the one you are becoming one with. Name the three most repeated self-sentences this week. Then ask, would the cross sign that sentence, or would Satan?

Question 2 · click to revealWhy is it easier to treat 'adultery' as someone else's bedroom sin than as your own agreement with a lie?

Why this question hits

Bedroom sin is visible and other people's. Mental covenant is private and ours. We prefer a gospel that polices neighbors.

A way to answer

Because physical adultery still lets us feel morally ahead of someone. Spiritual adultery exposes the respectable Christian who lives on the enemy's script: 'God is disappointed; you'll never change; you have to earn the next drop of love.' James 4 and 2 Corinthians 11 both use marriage language for that divided loyalty. The scandal is not only sex. The scandal is a bride who believes the rival.

Question 3 · click to revealWhat is one 'ring' the enemy has been holding out to you lately — a thought that wants a lifetime yes?

Why this question hits

The picture only helps if you put a real thought in the ring box: I'm too far gone. I'm only as good as my last week. God is tired of me.

A way to answer

Write the thought in a full sentence. Then write the Husband's sentence next to it (for example: 'There is therefore now no condemnation' — Romans 8:1; 'He hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified' — Hebrews 10:14). The study is not positive thinking. It is refusing to marry a sentence Jesus already buried.

Live it before the next session

  1. Before the next session, list five thoughts you have treated as 'just how I am' this month.
  2. Mark each one: Husband's voice, enemy's proposal, or my flesh recycling both.
  3. Pray Isaiah 54:5 out loud using your name: 'My Maker is my husband.'
