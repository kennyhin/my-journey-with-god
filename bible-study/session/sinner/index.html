Life and Death Are Sitting on Your Tongue

Satan does not need a new strategy if he can get you to say 'I'm a sinner' as your last name.

Watch this moment

What he said

He says this because of Proverbs 18:21. There is life and death in the power of the tongue. Matthew 12:37 says, For by the fruit of your lips, you shall be condemned. By the fruit of your lips, you shall be justified. So guess what? All Satan has to do is make you go, 'I'm a sinner.' Where do sinners go? They go to hell. That's agreeing with the world. So what is he saying here? Don't agree with what the world says about you. Agree with what the Bible says about me. The Bible says, Yes, all have fallen short, but we are justified through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus for free. It says you are holy, righteous, and perfected because of Jesus. He did not fail. You are new creatures. But what do we do? I don't feel like a new creature. I don't even act like a new creature. I fall every day. Right there, what are you trusting? Your thoughts.

He ties James's warning to speech. Proverbs says the tongue carries death and life. Jesus says your words will justify or condemn you. So the enemy's economy is efficient: get the bride to confess 'I am a sinner' as her identity, then let the logic run — sinners go to hell — which is the world's closing argument. The alternative is to agree with the whole Bible, not half of it: all have sinned, and those in Christ are justified freely, made holy, righteous, perfected by His offering, new creatures. Feelings and daily failure are real, but they are not the higher court. If you trust the thought-life over the Word, you have chosen a different witness.

Every loaded word

Not every “the” and “and” — the claims, pictures, and slang that carry the sermon.

“life and death in the power of the tongue”

Proverbs 18:21. Speech is not steam. It harvests. You eat the fruit of what you keep saying.

Why it matters. This is why identity talk matters. You are farming a future with your mouth, including the mouth inside the skull.

“by the fruit of your lips”

He is blending Matthew 12:37 ('by thy words') with the 'fruit of the mouth/lips' idiom from Proverbs 18:20 and Hebrews 13:15. The idea is the same: speech is fruit that reveals the tree and has courtroom weight.

Why it matters. Even the mix-up teaches his method: Scripture talks to Scripture. Just know the exact Matthew line is 'words,' and 'fruit of lips' lives next door in Proverbs/Hebrews.

“I'm a sinner. Where do sinners go? They go to hell.”

A logical chain the enemy loves. Premise 1: my name is Sinner. Premise 2: sinners are damned. Conclusion: I am not safely His.

Why it matters. The chain is broken at premise 1 for the person in Christ. You were a sinner by nature and practice; you are now a saint who still sins and confesses. Hell is not the Christian's last name.

“Don't agree with what the world says about you. Agree with what the Bible says about me.”

A change of court. The world, your feelings, and your last twenty-four hours are not the bench. Scripture is.

Why it matters. This is the practical center of the whole video. The rest is illustration.

“all have fallen short, but we are justified… for free”

Romans 3:23–24 without the reference. He refuses a Bible that stops at verse 23, which is how a lot of 'humility' preaches.

Why it matters. Half-verses are how spiritual adultery sounds conservative. 'The Bible says I'm a sinner' can be a way to dodge 'the Bible says I am justified freely.'

“holy, righteous, and perfected… He did not fail… new creatures”

Hebrews 10:14; 2 Corinthians 5:21; 5:17. The finished work is the Husband's performance review, not yours.

Why it matters. If Jesus failed, you should panic. If He did not, your feelings of failure are not allowed to rewrite His résumé.

“I don't feel like… I don't act like… I fall every day. What are you trusting? Your thoughts.”

He names the three idols of experience: affect, behavior, recurrence. Then he names the real trust issue.

Why it matters. The gospel never asked your nervous system to be the High Priest. Thoughts can be liars even when they have evidence.

Verses on the table

Proverbs 18:20–21 KJV

A man's belly shall be satisfied with the fruit of his mouth; and with the increase of his lips shall he be filled. Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof.

Word by word

  • fruit of his mouthSpeech grows something you later have to swallow. You do not get a different diet than your vocabulary.
  • Death and lifeNot 'rude and nice.' Covenant categories. Words can kill belonging or feed it.
  • power of the tongueYad, hand/power. The tongue is a tool with jurisdiction.
  • eat the fruitInevitable harvest. Love talking death, and death becomes dinner.
How the speaker uses this verse

Why James can say 'do you not know?' — they already knew the tongue was not a toy. Identity sentences are fruit.

Wider context — keep the verse honest

Proverbs also warns against lying lips and hasty speech. This is not a magic-formula prosperity tongue. It is moral and spiritual agriculture.

Matthew 12:36–37 KJV

But I say unto you, That every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof in the day of judgment. For by thy words thou shalt be justified, and by thy words thou shalt be condemned.

Word by word

  • idle wordCareless, unemployed speech — words you did not think were working. Jesus says they were.
  • give accountLogos: a reasoned account. The mouth has a court date.
  • justified / condemnedIn context, words reveal the heart's treasure (12:34–35). Jesus is not teaching salvation by slogans. He is teaching that speech is evidence of the tree.
How the speaker uses this verse

He hears: the words you agree to say about yourself participate in justification or condemnation. Handle this carefully: we are justified by faith in Christ; the mouth confesses that faith (Romans 10). A mantra cannot replace the cross. A mantra can contradict the cross until you live as if it failed.

Wider context — keep the verse honest

Matthew 12 is about the Pharisees' blasphemy and bad trees. The speaker applies 'your words matter' to self-talk. Fair pastorally; keep Jesus' warning about speaking against the Spirit in view too. The tongue is bigger than self-esteem.

Romans 3:23–24 KJV

For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God; Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.

Word by word

  • all have sinnedUniversal past and condition. No Christian skips this. The speaker says yes to it.
  • come shortPresent in English KJV cadence: we lack God's glory as a record. The gospel answers the lack; it does not deny it.
  • justifiedDeclared righteous. A courtroom word. This is the new last name.
  • freelyDorean: as a gift, without a bill. The opposite of try harder.
  • redemption that is in Christ JesusThe price sits in a Person, not in your improvement arc.
How the speaker uses this verse

His 'yes… but' Bible. Stop using verse 23 as a weapon against verse 24.

Wider context — keep the verse honest

This is the beating heart of Protestant (and Pauline) gospel. If this study does nothing else, let it glue 23 to 24 in your mouth.

2 Corinthians 5:17, 21; Hebrews 10:14; Romans 6:11 KJV

If any man be in Christ, he is a new creature… He hath made him to be sin for us… that we might be made the righteousness of God in him. / By one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified. / Reckon ye also yourselves to be dead indeed unto sin, but alive unto God.

Word by word

  • new creatureKaine ktisis: new creation. Ontology, not a gym membership.
  • made the righteousness of GodA gift-exchange at the cross. He wore our sin; we wear His rightness.
  • perfected for everTeteleioken: completed, brought to the goal. The offering worked. Ongoing sanctification does not mean the offering is still in committee.
  • ReckonLogizesthe: count it as so. Faith does math with God's numbers, not with the day's mood.
How the speaker uses this verse

These are the sentences he wants the bride to say I do to. 'He did not fail' is Hebrews 10 in street clothes.

Wider context — keep the verse honest

1 John 1:8 still stands: if we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves. The speaker is fighting identity-fusion with sin, not the practice of confession. A mature believer reckons themselves dead to sin and confesses when they do not act like it.

Sit with this

Try the question before you open the answer. The answer is a companion, not a score key.

Question 1 · click to revealIf your inner narrator was subpoenaed, would its testimony match Romans 3:24 or match a middle-school nickname that stuck?

Why this question hits

Most of us are still answering to a name we got from a parent, a pulpit, an ex, or a failure, not from the redemption in Christ Jesus.

A way to answer

Write the nickname. Then read Romans 3:23–24 out loud with your actual name in it: 'I, ____, have sinned… I, ____, am justified freely.' The point is not to fake feelings. The point is to change witnesses. Proverbs says you will eat the fruit of the narrator you keep hiring.

Question 2 · click to revealWhy is 'I don't feel like a new creature' such a persuasive god, and why is it still not allowed to sit on the throne?

Why this question hits

Feelings are honest sensors and terrible saviors. They report weather. They do not write covenants.

A way to answer

Feelings evolved to keep you alive in a body, and shame is loud because it thinks it is protecting you. The gospel is not 'never feel that.' It is 'do not marry that.' 2 Corinthians 5:17 is not a mood. Romans 6:11 says reckon — an accounting word — yourselves dead to sin. You can feel old and still be new, the way a freed slave can still flinch at a bell. Flinching is not the deed of ownership.

Question 3 · click to revealWhat would change in your week if you told the truth about your falls without letting the falls name you?

Why this question hits

People think those are the same act. They are not. One is confession. The other is a wedding.

A way to answer

You would still repent quickly, still repair what you broke, still hate the sin. You would not spend three days in a grave Jesus already emptied, trying to feel punished enough to deserve return. Hebrews 10:14 says the offering perfected the sanctified. 1 John 1:9 says confession meets a faithful and just cleanser. Together they mean: name the act, refuse the new last name, go home.

Live it before the next session

  1. For 48 hours, every time you start 'I am…' about your soul, finish it with a verse or do not finish it.
  2. Read Romans 3:21–26 slowly. Do not stop at 'all have sinned.'
  3. When you fail, practice a two-line prayer: 'That was sin. That is not my name.'
