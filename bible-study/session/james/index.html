James 4:4 Calls It by Name

Friendship with the world is not hanging out. It is taking the world's side against God.

Watch this moment

What he said

Go to James chapter 4. This is spiritual adultery. Watch. James 4:4 calls it by name, too. Ye adulterers and adulteresses. Know you not. Do you not know that the friendship of the world is enmity with God? Friendship here for this word in Koine Greek means agreeing. So agreeing with what the world says about me is enmity with God. Whosoever therefore will be a friend of the world is the enemy of God. So it's not just talking about sex. It is in agreeance with what the world says I am. Well, I'm a sinner. Sure, but what did Jesus die for? All of your sins. Yeah, but I still feel like a sinner because everyone else calls me a sinner. The world shows me I'm a sinner. The world says do better, try harder, so I'm going to live by that. James is speaking to the Jews here, the 12 tribes of Israel. Do you guys know what you're doing is wrong? That you guys are trying to live by the law, which literally brings sin and death, not the law of the Spirit and life. And you do know that if you break one, you break them all. And you're being friends with the world right now. That's the demonic doctrine.

James does not whisper. He names his readers adulterers and adulteresses because friendship with the world is hostility toward God. The speaker glosses the Greek word for friendship as 'agreeing,' then applies it: if you agree with the world's report that you are only a sinner who must do better and try harder, you have taken the world's side. He locates James's audience as the twelve tribes, and he hears their world-friendship as law-performance — a system that produces sin and death rather than the Spirit's life, and that condemns you wholly if you break one point. He calls that performance gospel a demonic doctrine.

Every loaded word

Not every “the” and “and” — the claims, pictures, and slang that carry the sermon.

“calls it by name”

James uses the actual word group for marital unfaithfulness. Spiritual betrayal is not a later preacher's invention.

Why it matters. You are not being extra when you treat divided loyalty as adultery. You are being canonical.

“Ye adulterers and adulteresses”

KJV language covering men and women. Some Greek manuscripts have moichalides (adulteresses) as the main word; the charge is corporate, not a gender slam.

Why it matters. The whole church can be the unfaithful wife. This is not 'those people over there.'

“friendship of the world is enmity with God”

Greek philia tou kosmou: affection/friendship with the world-order. Enmity is echthra — hostility, not a mild disagreement.

Why it matters. There is no Switzerland. A friend of that system is positioned as God's enemy. James is as sharp as the speaker sounds.

“Friendship here… in Koine Greek means agreeing”

He says 'Coney Greek' in the audio — he means Koine, the common Greek of the New Testament. Strictly, philia means friendship, love, affection. Agreement is an implication, not the dictionary's first gloss: you become a friend by sharing loves, values, and yes, verdicts.

Why it matters. Keep the grain, sand the overclaim. You do not need 'philia = agree' to preach his point. A friend takes the friend's side. If the world says you are only your failure, taking that side is philia.

“I'm a sinner… everyone else calls me a sinner… do better, try harder”

He is not denying Romans 3:23. He is attacking the identity that stays in the dock after the verdict of justification, and the religion of self-improvement that the world baptizes as humility.

Why it matters. There is a holy way to say 'I have sinned' (1 John 1:9) and a faithless way to say 'I am only a sinner' as if the cross bounced. This sermon is after the second.

“speaking to the Jews… 12 tribes… live by the law… break one, break them all”

James 1:1 addresses the twelve tribes. James 2:10 says one break makes you guilty of all. Romans 8:2 contrasts the law of sin and death with the law of the Spirit of life. He braids those texts into James 4.

Why it matters. His 'demonic doctrine' is not 'the Old Testament is evil.' It is using law as the living identity and the ladder back to God after Christ has already brought you home.

Verses on the table

Open each card for a word-level read, then how the speaker uses it, then the wider context so the verse is not kidnapped.

James 4:4

KJV
Ye adulterers and adulteresses, know ye not that the friendship of the world is enmity with God? whosoever therefore will be a friend of the world is the enemy of God.

  • know ye not

    James thinks they should already know. The speaker hears Proverbs and the law in that 'do you not know?'

  • friendship / philia

    A bond of affection. Not a handshake. A chosen closeness with the kosmos.

  • world / kosmos

    Not trees and oceans. The ordered human life that organizes itself without God — its loves, boasts, and verdicts (cf. 1 John 2:15–16).

  • enmity / enemy

    To side with that system is to stand opposite God. James will not let you be a double friend.

James 1:1 and 2:10

KJV
James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes which are scattered abroad, greeting. … For whosoever shall keep the whole law, and yet offend in one point, he is guilty of all.

  • twelve tribes

    A Jewish-Christian audience in the dispersion. The speaker is right that James is not writing a modern Gentile altar call first.

  • guilty of all

    Law as a single garment. Tear one seam, the whole is torn. That is why law-as-ladder cannot save or keep you.

Romans 8:1–2

KJV
There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus… For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus hath made me free from the law of sin and death.

  • no condemnation

    The court's gavel already fell, in your favor, in Christ.

  • law of the Spirit of life

    A different operating system: the Spirit produces life, not a new Moses you must climb.

  • law of sin and death

    The speaker's 'law which brings sin and death.' Paul can mean Torah-apart-from-Christ as a killing letter, and the sin-principle that uses commandment to slay (Romans 7).

  1. Read James 4:1–10 in one sitting. Circle every relationship word: friend, enemy, adulterers, submit, draw near.
  2. Replace one 'I'm just a sinner' this week with a full sentence: 'I sinned, I am forgiven, I am His.'
  3. Ask: is my current spiritual plan closer to Romans 8:2 or to 'do better, try harder'?
